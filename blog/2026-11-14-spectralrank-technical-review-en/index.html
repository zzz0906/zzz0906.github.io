    Spectral Rank and Step-Direction Dynamics in Language-Model Post-Training: Technical Review

    Review date 2026-11-14
    Author Zhongzhu Zhou
    Paper reviewed Spectral Rank and Step-Direction Dynamics in Language-Model Post-Training
    Paper authors Khurram Khalil and Khaza Anuarul Hoque
    arXiv 2611.11752
    Version and date v1, 2026-11-09

    The three measurements

    Khalil and Hoque ask how post-training moves a language model in weight space. Their study uses three objects. A matrix difference between an initialization and an endpoint has a singular spectrum. A sequence of intermediate checkpoints supports correlations between finite displacements. Finally, a paired training intervention asks whether changing the way advantages are computed changes those correlations. The three objects have different denominators, units and sources of uncertainty. I found that keeping them in view together makes the paper much easier to read.

    The practical trap is to see a small number labelled “rank” and budget a rank of that size for compression. In the selected OLMo-2-7B PPO attention query at layer zero, the paper reports stable rank 9.58 and 532 directions for 80% of the squared update energy. The second trap is to interpret the angle between two saved displacements as the angle between two optimizer updates. The public 7B PPO example compares finite windows of 60 release steps. The third trap is to treat a small, matched training experiment as an explanation of the entire public survey. Replacing group-relative advantages with a critic–GAE package makes the mean late cosine smaller in the matched study. The production pattern of consistently negative adjacent cosines remains a separate observation.

    Figure 1. Original schematic based on paper §§2–4. The endpoint uses one matrix difference, the trajectory uses a full-model checkpoint grid, and the intervention pairs training runs by seed.

    What the paper establishes

    The analytic result is a separation between stable rank and fixed-energy rank for a family of singular spectra. The observation from fourteen full decompositions is that these measures can be far apart for actual post-training updates. The survey then records negative adjacent cosines in every measured pair across nine public stage trajectories. Finally, the matched 1B intervention detects a drop in mean late-window cosine when the advantage package changes. These statements are respectively a theorem, a selected-case measurement, a descriptive survey, and an intervention in a specified setting. The paper’s numerical appendix is unusually relevant to all four: it documents a corrected initialization, a discarded coordinate sketch, sensitivity of threshold rank to approximate SVD, and the limits of the original controlled comparisons.

    The rest of this review uses my own equation numbering. Results from the paper are identified by its table, section, or appendix. The power-law sum, small vector examples, and arithmetic checks are my calculations. I did not run a model-training or task-evaluation experiment for this review.

    Prerequisites

    The model and the stage boundary

    An endpoint difference is a statement about two objects that share a parameterization. For a layer with weight shape mmm by nnn, the change is obtained by matching the same layer name, orientation, tensor dimensions and coordinates. In a weight-tied model, a full-model norm should include each independent parameter once. One-dimensional normalization weights contribute to the trajectory norm even though the per-matrix SVD study excludes them. A transposition preserves singular values, but it changes what the left singular vectors describe. That matters for the separate angle calculation on weight subspaces.

    A model may go from a base model to supervised fine-tuning (SFT), then direct preference optimization (DPO), then reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR). SFT trains on given conversations. DPO uses a fixed pair of preferred and dispreferred responses. RLVR samples responses from the evolving policy and scores them with a verifier. For the public OLMo-2-7B PPO endpoint, the immediate reference is DPO. For the author-run SFT endpoint, the reference is the corresponding base checkpoint. The spectra compare two kinds of endpoint in the paper’s setting, for which the objectives, exposure, reference and horizon all differ.

    Let WSW_SWS​, WDW_DWD​ and WRW_RWR​ be the SFT, DPO and RLVR endpoint matrices. Inserting the intermediate checkpoint gives

    WR−WS=(WR−WD)+(WD−WS).(1)W_R-W_S=(W_R-W_D)+(W_D-W_S). \tag{1}WR​−WS​=(WR​−WD​)+(WD​−WS​).(1)

    This identity is linear in weights; singular values of the sum depend on both spectra and their orientation. If the two changes use the same leading left and right directions, they can add or cancel. If their supports in both singular bases are disjoint, the sum can carry both spectra. For interior trajectory differences, subtracting the same reference from both endpoints cancels exactly in real arithmetic. The first window can still change because a different initial checkpoint changes the window itself. Subtract the source values after widening storage precision when small differences are the quantity of interest.

    SFT, PPO, group-relative advantages and GAE

    The SFT runs in this paper supervise all retained non-padding conversation tokens that have a preceding token. A response-only SFT run changes that mask and can move weights differently. The normalization divides by the number of supervised tokens. At a fixed example count, tokenization and length can still change how many gradient terms the model sees. The cross-family benchmark matches 64 examples per optimizer step and a common schedule, with token exposure left model dependent.

    For RLVR, an advantage assigns credit or blame relative to a baseline. Proximal policy optimization uses a token probability ratio between the current policy and the policy used when sampling. For positive advantage, the clipped objective stops rewarding a ratio increase after the upper clipping bound. For negative advantage, the incentive to decrease the ratio saturates at the lower bound. The product uses the same sampled action under both probabilities. The reference-policy penalty and several passes over a rollout add further dependence on past parameters and sample reuse. Displacements therefore reflect the optimizer, the rollout and the training schedule as well as the objective. The public stage labels refer to released training recipes. The two local arms deliberately share a clipped actor objective, so I refer to them by their advantage packages.

    For group-relative advantages, the paper standardizes the eight outcome rewards for each prompt with their population standard deviation and a stabilizing constant. It broadcasts one standardized value to all retained tokens in a response. For a binary reward group with exactly one success and seven failures, the mean is 1/81/81/8 and the population standard deviation is 7/8\sqrt{7}/87​/8. Ignoring the stabilizer for this example, the success receives 7\sqrt{7}7​ and a failure receives −1/7-1/\sqrt{7}−1/7​. Their response-level sum is zero. Longer trajectories still occupy more terms in the token-weighted actor objective, and a response without EOS can receive a different reward in the paper’s protocol.

    The critic estimates a state value V(sj)V(s_j)V(sj​). Using reward rjr_jrj​, discount γ\gammaγ and trace decay λ\lambdaλ, the paper first forms a temporal-difference residual and then accumulates residuals backward from the terminal token. Let JJJ index the terminal token and set its successor value to zero.

    δj=rj+γV(sj+1)−V(sj),Aj=∑ℓ=0J−j(γλ)ℓδj+ℓ=δj+γλAj+1,AJ+1=0.(2)\begin{aligned} \delta_j &= r_j+\gamma V(s_{j+1})-V(s_j),\\ A_j &= \sum_{\ell=0}^{J-j}(\gamma\lambda)^\ell\delta_{j+\ell} =\delta_j+\gamma\lambda A_{j+1},\qquad A_{J+1}=0 . \end{aligned} \tag{2}δj​Aj​​=rj​+γV(sj+1​)−V(sj​),=ℓ=0∑J−j​(γλ)ℓδj+ℓ​=δj​+γλAj+1​,AJ+1​=0.​(2)

    Separate the first term of the sum and factor out γλ\gamma\lambdaγλ to get the recurrence. When λ=1\lambda=1λ=1, the intermediate value terms telescope and the residual sum reduces to the discounted return less the starting value. At the paper’s γ=1,λ=0.95\gamma=1,\lambda=0.95γ=1,λ=0.95, more distant residuals count less. With a zero critic and a reward of one at the terminal token, the raw advantage ten positions before that token is about 0.599. The controlled critic arm whitens these quantities across all retained rollout tokens before actor updates. Its own target uses the unwhitened return estimate. Thus changing arms changes both the granularity of credit and the population used for normalization. A variance-reduction argument for a state baseline alone will miss those details.

    Approximate truncation and functional error

    For Δk=∑i=1kσiuivi⊤\Delta_k=\sum_{i=1}^k\sigma_i u_i v_i^\topΔk​=∑i=1k​σi​ui​vi⊤​, orthonormality makes the truncated expansion the best rank-kkk approximation in Frobenius norm. Combining this property with the orthogonal residual gives

    ∥Δ−Δk∥F2∥Δ∥F2=∑i>kσi2∑iσi2=1−Ek.(5)\frac{\|\Delta-\Delta_k\|_F^2}{\|\Delta\|_F^2} =\frac{\sum_{i>k}\sigma_i^2}{\sum_i\sigma_i^2}=1-E_k . \tag{5}∥Δ∥F2​∥Δ−Δk​∥F2​​=∑i​σi2​∑i>k​σi2​​=1−Ek​.(5)

    A threshold of 80% therefore leaves a 20% squared residual, or about 44.7% relative Frobenius norm. I keep both units explicit. A system allocating adapter storage from “80%” may interpret it as a bound on the norm or on an accuracy drop. A reconstruction threshold alone bounds neither model loss nor needed adapter training rank. The numerator and denominator describe only weight-space geometry. Even within one layer, a unit of error in a direction seldom excited by inputs can have a very different effect from the same norm of error along a common feature.

    Algorithm 1. Measure the spectrum of a stage update

    This is measurement pseudocode derived from paper §3.3 and Appendix E. The uniform interface accommodates both the exact calculation and a screening pass.

    1  Load initial and final tensors for the same named matrix.
2  Check shape and stored orientation; widen both before subtraction.
3  Form the update and its squared Frobenius norm in FP64.
4  If the norm is zero, record a zero update with undefined ratios.
5  For a wide matrix, transpose only for spectrum measurement.
6  For a tall matrix, take reduced QR and keep the triangular factor.
7  Compute all singular values of the reduced matrix in FP64.
8  Accumulate squared values in order; divide by the Frobenius total.
9  Report stable rank, requested prefix energies and the first crossing.
10 In a screening pass, log the sketch budget and threshold uncertainty.

    For a tall matrix Δ=QR\Delta=QRΔ=QR with orthonormal columns in QQQ, the Gram matrix obeys Δ⊤Δ=R⊤Q⊤QR=R⊤R\Delta^\top\Delta=R^\top Q^\top Q R=R^\top RΔ⊤Δ=R⊤Q⊤QR=R⊤R. The singular values are square roots of the eigenvalues of this common Gram matrix, so a full SVD of RRR preserves the spectrum. For a 100,352×4,096100{,}352\times4{,}096100,352×4,096 embedding, the QR input has about 411 million entries. FP64 storage for a dense input alone is about 3.29 GB in decimal units, whereas RRR has 16.78 million entries or about 134 MB. QR still reads the original matrix and costs on the order of mn2mn^2mn2. Its benefit is reducing the shape passed to the subsequent SVD and avoiding direct eigendecomposition of an explicitly squared-condition-number Gram matrix.

    A randomized method projects onto a sampled low-dimensional range. Extra columns reduce sensitivity to a hard cutoff and power iterations emphasize dominant directions. This is attractive when only a leading-value screening estimate is needed. A slowly decaying tail near the cutoff creates a harder problem: a good estimate of the maximum need not imply a good sum over 256 or 512 near-cutoff directions. An independent Frobenius total helps isolate prefix error. Using a sketched total as well can hide the energy left outside the sketch.

    Algorithm 2. Accumulate the displacement Gram matrix

    This measurement procedure follows the full-coordinate protocol in paper §3.3. Materializing one full concatenated vector for every checkpoint is unnecessary for the dot products.

    1  Freeze checkpoint order, width, units and immediate reference.
2  Match model tensors and count each tied parameter once.
3  Initialize a square FP64 Gram matrix for all reported windows.
4  For each matched tensor, iterate blocks of coordinates.
5      Read those coordinates from each checkpoint in FP64.
6      Subtract consecutive endpoints; stack one row per window.
7      Add the block times its transpose to the Gram matrix.
8  For each lag, divide entries by the product of diagonal norms.
9  Report pair counts and mark zero-norm comparisons undefined.
10 For adjacent pairs, also report the signed projection coefficient.

    A block with CCC coordinates and JJJ windows needs O(JC)O(JC)O(JC) temporary values. Updating a full Gram matrix over PPP coordinates uses O(PJ2)O(PJ^2)O(PJ2) arithmetic and O(J2)O(J^2)O(J2) output. Computing only selected lags reduces arithmetic to O(PJL)O(PJL)O(PJL) for LLL requested lags. The full matrix additionally supports reaggregation, endpoint norm checks, and comparisons of several separations without rereading every coordinate. The paper checks online and saved cosines for the six local runs to within 1.4×10−121.4\times10^{-12}1.4×10−12 absolute. In Appendix G, the reported in-memory versus checkpoint Gram comparison on Mistral uses more than 7.24 billion coordinates. My small generated matrix check agrees with direct multiplication to 1.71×10−131.71\times10^{-13}1.71×10−13 absolute and checks the chunk identity, independent of the training data.

    Dependence on checkpoint storage

    Suppose a saved vector θ~t\tilde\theta_tθ~t​ differs from the underlying vector by quantization error ete_tet​. Then D~j=Dj+ej−ej−1\tilde D_j=D_j+e_j-e_{j-1}D~j​=Dj​+ej​−ej−1​. The errors in adjacent windows share one endpoint with opposite sign. This gives a mechanism for negative observed dot products even when the latent motions are weakly aligned. I return to a variance calculation in the critical analysis. Here the protocol consequence is that FP64 subtraction preserves the available difference between saved numbers. Bits already lost at checkpoint time cannot be recovered.

    The paper’s comparison of BF16 versus FP32 parameter and optimizer-state storage in Llama actually changes numerical training, including rounding along the optimization path. This differs from measuring the same latent FP32 trajectory twice after two choices of serialization. The comparison is valuable for showing configuration dependence and has limited leverage for deciding between these two mechanisms. Earlier coordinate sketch results in Appendix F are also explicitly withdrawn. A sampler that admits no coordinates after the first two million estimates the geometry of the earliest coordinates in enumeration order. Full chunked accumulation is feasible for the number of saved windows used here and removes that source of bias.

    A path-level statistic can help interpret whether many opposed steps add up to a small or a substantial displacement. Using the Gram matrix, the squared length of the total update over JJJ windows equals the sum of all diagonal entries plus twice the upper-triangular sum. In particular, negative nearest-neighbor products alone cannot determine its size. Longer-separation terms and window norms enter. I would report the endpoint norm relative to total path length along with adjacent cosines so a reader can distinguish a nearly stationary oscillation from steady drift with local corrections.

    The matched advantage intervention

    Shared schedule and changed credit assignment

    Both arms start from OLMo-2-0425-1B-DPO. Each of the three paired seeds has a shared prompt stream from RLVR-GSM. A rollout has eight prompts with eight responses each, then four passes through eight-response minibatches. There are eight minibatches in each pass. Hence a rollout advances the actor by 32 optimizer updates and produces 64 responses, and a 60-rollout geometry window corresponds to 1,920 updates and 3,840 responses. The response and prompt caps are 2,048 tokens each. Learning rate 4×10−74\times10^{-7}4×10−7 decays linearly over 360 rollout batches. The reference KL coefficient is 0.05 and ratio clipping is 0.2; actor trainable parameters and optimizer states are FP32 with BF16 autocast. A correct response receives 1, an incorrect response 0 and a missing EOS −10-10−10 in the stated reward configuration.

    Both arms reuse the actor schedule while switching the credit and normalization protocol. Group-relative training broadcasts one standardized outcome advantage per response. Critic–GAE uses a separate critic backbone and scalar head, a temporal return, tokenwise estimates, and rollout-level whitening. The critic’s value loss and optimizer add compute. Sharing prompt streams controls task sampling at the prompt level; actions, sequence lengths, KL exposure and actual generated token counts diverge. Paper Table 8 gives group versus critic token totals of 5.287M versus 5.832M at seed 42, 4.780M versus 5.861M at seed 43, and 5.072M versus 4.777M at seed 44. The contrast estimates the effect of switching the package under matched rollout and update budgets. The critic’s extra learning and changing token distribution are part of that intervention.

    This study has six runs but three paired statistical units. A distribution-free sign test illustrates how little information that contains about a population median. Under exchangeable independent signs and a continuous null centered at zero, three differences all having the same sign gives probability 2/23=0.252/2^3=0.252/23=0.25 for the two-sided extremity. The Student interval uses distances and modeling assumptions in addition to signs. The two summaries answer differently specified questions. I would keep the raw three seed differences visible alongside the interval, and repeat the paired intervention with more seeds before using the magnitude to select a production algorithm.

    Training health and scope

    The fixed 32-example greedy diagnostic starts at 20 correct and ends between 16 and 20 across the six runs. Group-relative runs give 32 EOS completions each on that diagnostic, whereas the critic arm gives 27, 28 and 29. Training cap-hit rates are lower in the critic runs, so lower cap-hit during sampling and lower EOS completion in the greedy diagnostic coexist. They are different measures on different datasets and protocols. Appendix B reports finite-update checks and a correction from earlier truncated response-cap experiments. Neither the paired interval nor the health counts establishes an improvement in task quality. A larger held-out set, explicit reporting of truncated and invalid samples, and return distributions by prompt difficulty would make the geometry easier to connect to behavior.

    Numerical validation and measurement choices

    An unresolved threshold has two possible explanations

    The paper’s rank-256 stress case is the SFT layer-16 query update. Full decomposition gives k0.8=255k_{0.8}=255k0.8​=255 and E256=0.80027920E_{256}=0.80027920E256​=0.80027920. Stable rank with the accurate denominator has at most about 0.0088% relative error in the baseline comparison. The original normalization increases its worst error to 2.38%.

    Measurement checklist for a follow-up

    A paper-faithful comparison starts with a manifest of checkpoint labels, the immediately preceding checkpoint, tensor orientation, dtype, and the exposure unit. For each model list which weights are tied and which vectors enter the full measure. To compare SFT and RLVR keep objective details, mask, initialization, optimizer, precision, schedule and training horizon alongside the figures. This lets a reader explain an apparent contradiction without assuming it is a mistake. Appendix F documents why the initial PPO reference is DPO and why old claims from the limited sketch and unmatched experiments were withdrawn.

    For the spectral record I would carry dimensionality, absolute update norm, norm ratio to the reference, leading value, stable rank, energies at several explicit rank budgets and exact threshold rank or a sensitivity interval. Record approximation settings and whether the trace was normalized against total energy. Run deterministic small checks first: diagonal ground truth, an orthogonal change of basis, an all-zero case, and a tall matrix whose direct spectrum agrees with its reduced QR spectrum. The local random-matrix QR check in this review gave identical singular values at displayed precision. A high-aspect-ratio embedding test can then exercise memory and timing independently of end-task evaluation.

    For direction I would retain the Gram matrix, displacement norms, every pair’s window labels and the final cosine and projection readings. A known two-window example should match both vector arithmetic and block computation. A wider grid should use a stated offset and exactly the endpoint coverage requested by the comparison. For the intervention, report seed-level means, per-seed differences, token and optimizer exposure, the cap-hit rates and end-task uncertainty on a larger diagnostic. The paper reports its public inventory in Appendix D and local protocol in Appendix B. The counts provide useful checks even when a larger study will eventually change the schedule.

    Limitations

    The theorem establishes possibility under a specified power law. There is no fit in the paper showing that each selected spectrum obeys that law over all directions. Stable rank and threshold rank are scale invariant, so the difference between a barely moving matrix and a strongly changed matrix disappears unless its absolute norm is also reported. Fourteen matrices cannot map the variation over all 226 matrices of one model, much less across architectures. The two 7B endpoints also start from different checkpoints and have different training histories. The numerical results establish where the gap occurs in the selected sample.

    The survey covers OLMo releases on the grids at which checkpoints are available. Native widths vary, release step labels differ in exposure, and stages share lineages. Measured signs are robust descriptions of those saved sequences. Generalization to other methods, model families or timescales requires additional trajectories. The SFT counterexamples show that negative cosines can arise under a supervised objective. The precision follow-up changes several numerical properties, including optimizer-state precision. The matched intervention isolates the advantage package under its own actor schedule. It changes a fitted critic, temporal credit, whitening population and realized token counts together. Three seeds in one 1B setting and a 32-example task diagnostic provide limited statistical and behavioral resolution.

    Critical analysis

    Test whether the error can account for the lag profile

    An adjacent pair shares one subtraction endpoint. With independent, zero-mean storage errors eje_jej​ across checkpoints and independent of every true displacement, the expected additional adjacent dot product is negative. Expand the product of the error differences. Three of four terms vanish by independence; the shared ej−1e_{j-1}ej−1​ term remains with a minus sign.

    E⟨ej−ej−1,ej−1−ej−2⟩=−E∥ej−1∥22.(11)\begin{aligned} \mathbb E\langle e_j-e_{j-1}, e_{j-1}-e_{j-2}\rangle &=-\mathbb E\|e_{j-1}\|_2^2 . \end{aligned} \tag{11}E⟨ej​−ej−1​,ej−1​−ej−2​⟩​=−E∥ej−1​∥22​.​(11)

    The same idealized mechanism contributes nothing in expectation at lag two or higher because no endpoints are shared. This calculation concerns dot products, with cosine denominators also affected. Quantization errors are generally neither independent nor independent of the trajectory. The derivation is a diagnostic for designing measurements, not an estimate of the paper’s bias. The near-zero lag-two and lag-three means in the public PPO example make the alternative testable. Measure exactly the same higher-precision checkpoints before and after controlled rounding, compare lag profiles on both, and stratify by update-to-roundoff ratio. Include resampling or staggered checkpoint grids at several widths to separate one-endpoint artifacts from genuine dynamics that decay over time.

    Test compression on activations and task outcomes

    I would separate two uses of low rank. The first approximates an already-trained full update. The second trains inside the family of low-rank factors and may find a different endpoint. For the first use, the Frobenius criterion assumes all input directions count equally. If xxx is the layer input with second moment C=E[xx⊤]C=\mathbb E[xx^\top]C=E[xx⊤], and M=Δ−ΔkM=\Delta-\Delta_kM=Δ−Δk​, linear output distortion expands as

    E∥Mx∥22=Etr⁡(Mxx⊤M⊤)=tr⁡(MCM⊤)=∥MC1/2∥F2.(12)\mathbb E\|Mx\|_2^2 =\mathbb E\operatorname{tr}(Mxx^\top M^\top) =\operatorname{tr}(MCM^\top) =\|MC^{1/2}\|_F^2. \tag{12}E∥Mx∥22​=Etr(Mxx⊤M⊤)=tr(MCM⊤)=∥MC1/2∥F2​.(12)

    A broad tail can be inexpensive to remove on one prompt distribution and consequential on another. Conversely, a small tail aligned with frequently activated features can produce disproportionately large output distortion. A credible ablation should allocate equal total factor storage across recipes and compare magnitude-based, fixed-energy and activation-weighted truncations on both training-like and shifted prompts. Keep per-layer absolute norms and compare output error, verifier pass rate, calibration and deployment cost. Then train LoRA at a few matched budgets from the same immediate reference. An experimentally successful low-rank path would answer a different question from the best Frobenius approximation of a full update. The paper supplies a useful pretest for estimating where these approaches could disagree.

    Separate correction, noise, and credit assignment

    The paired study changes a package. A follow-up can vary the learned baseline and whitening population separately while retaining terminal rewards, prompt pools and actor schedule. Compare group-relative, a leave-one-out prompt baseline, a state-value baseline with full-return advantages, and GAE at multiple trace decays. Run both an on-policy paired protocol, which estimates the effect of the training regime including subsequent behavior, and a frozen-rollout comparison, which asks how much the immediate gradient direction changes on identical token sequences. Record raw gradients, preconditioned updates and checkpoint displacement cosines as distinct quantities. Observing which stage of the pipeline introduces anti-alignment would narrow the plausible mechanism.

    I would also split global dot products by tensor family. The global numerator is a sum of block inner products, while normalization uses global norms. Averaging the cosines of layers would change the estimand because it gives a low-energy layer equal weight. Reporting signed block contributions and their shares of each displacement’s energy would show whether a handful of tensors dominate. Combined with the same checkpoint grids and the storage-precision experiment, this gives a way to assess whether attention, embeddings or the MLP are driving a geometric effect. The existing full-coordinate measurements make this a natural extension of the analysis.

    Conclusion

    The paper’s cleanest lesson is about choosing the quantity before estimating it. The leading singular value determines how stable rank is normalized. A cumulative threshold asks how far the tail must be traversed for a chosen residual. The Gram matrix asks whether finite moves on a stated grid point together, and the paired intervention shows how much that reading changed under one credit-assignment package. I would use full-SVD energy or certified bounds when allocating irreversible compression budgets, report multiple widths and actual norms when interpreting displacement cosines, and test any claimed effect on quality on the task itself.
