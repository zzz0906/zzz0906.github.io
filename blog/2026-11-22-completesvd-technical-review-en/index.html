    Complete-SVD: Choosing What a Compressed Language Model Should Preserve

    Review date: 2026-11-22 (UTC)
    Review author: Zhongzhu Zhou
    Paper reviewed: Complete-SVD: A Functional Norm Approach to Low-Rank LLM Compression
    Paper authors: Alper Kalle, Theo Rudkiewicz, Mohamed-Oumar Ouerfelli, Mohamed Tamaazousti
    arXiv / DOI: 2611.26059 / 10.48550/arXiv.2611.26059
    Version and date: v1, 19 November 2026

    1. The question behind the factorization

    I read Complete-SVD as a paper about choosing an error metric. A truncated SVD is an optimal answer to a particular matrix approximation problem. It does not, by itself, identify which approximation problem preserves a language model. A large weight direction can be rarely excited, or its output can be suppressed by later layers. Conversely, a modest local error can propagate into many future token predictions. The paper moves the comparison all the way to the model’s logits, then derives a tractable low-rank approximation under that comparison [1, Sections 1–2].

    Its objective gives equal weight to every logit coordinate. This differs from preserving the observed token’s log-probability, and from preserving the model’s predictive distribution through KL divergence. The distinction matters empirically. With WikiText-2 calibration and uniform ranks, Complete-SVD has the lowest C4 and PTB perplexity in all six tested model/ratio configurations. The matched KL-SVD counterpart generally fits WikiText-2 better and has higher average zero-shot accuracy. These are different operating preferences, rather than a single ranking of compression quality.

    The second contribution makes that metric affordable. Instead of differentiating every vocabulary entry at every output position separately, the authors use the linear language-model head and randomized backward probes. Independent signs across positions remove cross-position terms in expectation. The resulting output-side Gram estimator is positive semidefinite on every draw and unbiased conditional on the fixed calibration set. Those two properties do not establish that the compressed network is unbiased, or that the Kronecker approximation is exact.

    I read all 23 pages of v1, including Appendices A–H, the 15 tables and three figures. Numerical results below belong to that paper unless explicitly labeled as my arithmetic or an illustrative example. I did not train a compressed model or run an inference benchmark. My small calculations check mathematical identities and denominators; they are not a reproduction of the reported model results.

    2. Prerequisites: rank, parameter budgets, and geometry

    For consistency, I use column-vector activations. Let a∈Rdaa\in\mathbb R^{d_a}a∈Rda​ be the target layer’s input, W∈Rds×daW\in\mathbb R^{d_s\times d_a}W∈Rds​×da​ its weight, s=Was=Was=Wa its output, and qqq the remaining network. The logits are o=q(s)∈RVo=q(s)\in\mathbb R^Vo=q(s)∈RV. The paper sometimes places the input dimension on the left of the weight; transposing its expressions gives the convention used here. This choice matters when implementing the algebra on paper: the input covariance and output Gram have different dimensions and cannot exchange sides.

    A rank-rrr replacement writes W^=BA\widehat W=BAW=BA, with A∈Rr×daA\in\mathbb R^{r\times d_a}A∈Rr×da​ and B∈Rds×rB\in\mathbb R^{d_s\times r}B∈Rds​×r. It stores r(da+ds)r(d_a+d_s)r(da​+ds​) scalar parameters instead of dadsd_ad_sda​ds​. Thus the paper’s ordinary factor-storage compression ratio is a retained-parameter ratio:

    ρ=r(da+ds)dads,r≤⌊ρdadsda+ds⌋.(1)\rho=\frac{r(d_a+d_s)}{d_ad_s},\qquad r\le\left\lfloor\frac{\rho d_ad_s}{d_a+d_s}\right\rfloor.\tag{1}ρ=da​ds​r(da​+ds​)​,r≤⌊da​+ds​ρda​ds​​⌋.(1)

    For a square 4096×40964096\times40964096×4096 projection, ρ=0.7\rho=0.7ρ=0.7 means approximately r=1433r=1433r=1433 before any alignment rule, not 0.7×40960.7\times40960.7×4096 retained singular directions. One factor pair at that rank stores 11,739,136 parameters versus 16,777,216 originally. The rank allocator can additionally require multiples of eight. Keeping a fraction of singular directions and keeping the same fraction of factor parameters are distinct budgets.

    Ordinary truncated SVD solves min⁡rank(W^)≤r∥W−W^∥F2\min_{\mathrm{rank}(\widehat W)\le r}\|W-\widehat W\|_F^2minrank(W)≤r​∥W−W∥F2​. It follows from orthogonal invariance and the Eckart–Young result: in singular coordinates, the best rank-rrr approximation keeps the largest rrr singular values, leaving squared error ∑i>rσi2\sum_{i>r}\sigma_i^2∑i>r​σi2​. Optimality concerns that norm, not perplexity. Activation-aware methods replace raw weight error with expected local-output error. With the uncentered second moment Ca=E[aa⊤]C_a=\mathbb E[aa^\top]Ca​=E[aa⊤],

    E∥ΔWa∥22=Etr⁡(ΔWaa⊤ΔW⊤)=∥ΔWCa1/2∥F2,ΔW=W^−W.(2)\mathbb E\|\Delta W a\|_2^2 =\mathbb E\operatorname{tr}(\Delta W aa^\top\Delta W^\top) =\|\Delta W C_a^{1/2}\|_F^2,\quad \Delta W=\widehat W-W.\tag{2}E∥ΔWa∥22​=Etr(ΔWaa⊤ΔW⊤)=∥ΔWCa1/2​∥F2​,ΔW=W−W.(2)

    The term is often called an input covariance, but no mean subtraction is implied by this definition. Centering activations would change the objective unless the mean contribution were handled separately. Complete-SVD adds a second metric, on the output side, to account for the downstream network.

    A final prerequisite is local curvature. A quadratic output cost δo⊤Kδo\delta o^\top K\delta oδo⊤Kδo specifies which perturbations matter through a positive semidefinite kernel KKK. The same layer and calibration inputs can produce very different rank choices when KKK changes. That is the organizing idea connecting the apparently different methods in this paper.

    3. Three output metrics, three notions of fidelity

    Let p=softmax⁡(o)p=\operatorname{softmax}(o)p=softmax(o) and let yyy be the observed next token. Differentiating its negative log-probability gives ∇oℓ=p−ey\nabla_o\ell=p-e_y∇o​ℓ=p−ey​. Squaring the leading loss change produces the rank-one empirical-Fisher kernel. Averaging instead over a label sampled from ppp yields the true Fisher:

    Kemp=(p−ey)(p−ey)⊤,EY∼p[(eY−p)(eY−p)⊤]=∑vpvevev⊤−pp⊤=Diag⁡(p)−pp⊤=KKL,KComplete=IV.(3)\begin{aligned} K_{\mathrm{emp}}&=(p-e_y)(p-e_y)^\top,\\ \mathbb E_{Y\sim p}[(e_Y-p)(e_Y-p)^\top] &=\sum_vp_ve_ve_v^\top-pp^\top =\operatorname{Diag}(p)-pp^\top=K_{\mathrm{KL}},\\ K_{\mathrm{Complete}}&=I_V. \end{aligned}\tag{3}Kemp​EY∼p​[(eY​−p)(eY​−p)⊤]KComplete​​=(p−ey​)(p−ey​)⊤,=v∑​pv​ev​ev⊤​−pp⊤=Diag(p)−pp⊤=KKL​,=IV​.​(3)

    The KL kernel comes from the second-order expansion DKL(p∥p^)=12δo⊤KKLδo+o(∥δo∥2)D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|\widehat p)=\tfrac12\delta o^\top K_{\mathrm{KL}}\delta o+o(\|\delta o\|^2)DKL​(p∥p​)=21​δo⊤KKL​δo+o(∥δo∥2). The constant one-half does not change the minimizer of a fixed-rank quadratic objective. It does matter if one combines objectives or compares their unnormalized numerical values. The empirical-Fisher expression preserves a particular observed-label score to leading order. It is not generally an unbiased estimate of the true Fisher when labels come from the corpus rather than the teacher distribution [1, Appendices B–C].

    Figure 2: Original mathematical visualization using illustrative probabilities 0.55, 0.20, 0.10, 0.148 and 0.002. The observed label is Paris. These matrices are calculated examples, not measured model statistics. Their off-diagonal entries explain why a list of per-token weights alone is insufficient to describe the KL metric.

    The empirical kernel constrains one direction for each context. KL respects changes in probability mass and discounts directions that have little effect on the teacher’s current distribution. Complete-SVD penalizes an equal-sized change to a rare token logit just as much as one to a likely token logit. A plausible interpretation is that the latter is less tied to the teacher’s confidence on a narrow calibration domain. The experiments are consistent with that interpretation, but they do not prove that rare-token preservation causes the cross-corpus gains.

    There is also a deliberate overconstraint. Adding c1c\mathbf1c1 to all logits leaves softmax unchanged. KL assigns zero cost to that direction, whereas uniform-logit matching assigns Vc2Vc^2Vc2. The stronger metric may serve as a useful regularizer, but it also spends capacity preserving a quantity irrelevant to next-token probabilities. I return to this boundary in the critical analysis. The word “complete” identifies the full logit-space norm; it should not be read as a guarantee of complete task fidelity.

    4. From the full network to a weighted SVD

    For a fixed context, first-order linearization gives δo≈JsΔWa\delta o\approx J_s\Delta W aδo≈Js​ΔWa, where Js=∂o/∂sJ_s=\partial o/\partial sJs​=∂o/∂s. Writing v=vec⁡(ΔW)v=\operatorname{vec}(\Delta W)v=vec(ΔW) in column-major order gives δo≈(a⊤⊗Js)v\delta o\approx(a^\top\otimes J_s)vδo≈(a⊤⊗Js​)v. Consequently the exact quadratic term for the linearized network is

    E∥δo∥22≈v⊤GWv,GW=E[(aa⊤)⊗(Js⊤Js)].(4)\mathbb E\|\delta o\|_2^2 \approx v^\top G_Wv,\qquad G_W=\mathbb E[(aa^\top)\otimes(J_s^\top J_s)].\tag{4}E∥δo∥22​≈v⊤GW​v,GW​=E[(aa⊤)⊗(Js⊤​Js​)].(4)

    This single-site expression establishes the matrix orientation. With a weight shared across sequence positions, the exact parameter Jacobian also contains interactions between those uses; the tokenwise separability adopted for the practical factors is part of the structural approximation. Equation (8) below retains all causal output descendants in the output-side factor.

    This is a large matrix: a layer with dadsd_ad_sda​ds​ parameters has a Gram of size (dads)2(d_ad_s)^2(da​ds​)2. More importantly, the expectation of a product in Equation (4) does not generally factor into a product of expectations. Complete-SVD makes a Kronecker structural approximation,

    GW≈Ca⊗Cg,Cg=E[Js⊤Js],v⊤(Ca⊗Cg)v=∥Cg1/2ΔWCa1/2∥F2.(5)G_W\approx C_a\otimes C_g,\qquad C_g=\mathbb E[J_s^\top J_s],\qquad v^\top(C_a\otimes C_g)v =\|C_g^{1/2}\Delta W C_a^{1/2}\|_F^2.\tag{5}GW​≈Ca​⊗Cg​,Cg​=E[Js⊤​Js​],v⊤(Ca​⊗Cg​)v=∥Cg1/2​ΔWCa1/2​∥F2​.(5)

    The last identity follows by expanding the trace as tr⁡(CgΔWCaΔW⊤)\operatorname{tr}(C_g\Delta W C_a\Delta W^\top)tr(Cg​ΔWCa​ΔW⊤) and using vec⁡(X)⊤(B⊗A)vec⁡(X)=tr⁡(AXBX⊤)\operatorname{vec}(X)^\top(B\otimes A)\operatorname{vec}(X)=\operatorname{tr}(AX B X^\top)vec(X)⊤(B⊗A)vec(X)=tr(AXBX⊤) for symmetric BBB. This makes the dimensions and the two metric roles explicit. Input whitening highlights excited input directions; output whitening highlights local-output directions whose errors are amplified at the logits.

    There are two approximation layers. Taylor error disappears as the perturbation shrinks under the usual differentiability conditions. The discrepancy between GWG_WGW​ and Ca⊗CgC_a\otimes C_gCa​⊗Cg​ is a structural discrepancy in curvature. Its effect on an absolute quadratic cost shrinks with ∥ΔW∥2\|\Delta W\|^2∥ΔW∥2, but its relative directional distortion need not disappear. Increasing the number of random probes cannot remove that structural discrepancy. Appendix A explicitly separates these issues; it would be misleading to group both under “estimation noise.”

    Assume for now that the two metric matrices are positive definite. Define P=Cg1/2P=C_g^{1/2}P=Cg1/2​, Q=Ca1/2Q=C_a^{1/2}Q=Ca1/2​, and M=PWQM=PWQM=PWQ. Multiplication by invertible P,QP,QP,Q preserves rank, so the change of variable X=PW^QX=P\widehat WQX=PWQ maps every feasible rank-rrr matrix to another feasible rank-rrr matrix. The optimization becomes an ordinary SVD problem:

    min⁡rank⁡(W^)≤r∥P(W−W^)Q∥F2=min⁡rank⁡(X)≤r∥M−X∥F2,M=UΣV⊤,W^=P−1UrΣrVr⊤Q−1.(6)\begin{aligned} \min_{\operatorname{rank}(\widehat W)\le r} \|P(W-\widehat W)Q\|_F^2 &=\min_{\operatorname{rank}(X)\le r}\|M-X\|_F^2,\\ M&=U\Sigma V^\top,\\ \widehat W&=P^{-1}U_r\Sigma_rV_r^\top Q^{-1}. \end{aligned}\tag{6}rank(W)≤rmin​∥P(W−W)Q∥F2​MW​=rank(X)≤rmin​∥M−X∥F2​,=UΣV⊤,=P−1Ur​Σr​Vr⊤​Q−1.​(6)

    A balanced factorization splits Σr\Sigma_rΣr​ into two square roots. Then B=P−1UrΣr1/2B=P^{-1}U_r\Sigma_r^{1/2}B=P−1Ur​Σr1/2​ and A=Σr1/2Vr⊤Q−1A=\Sigma_r^{1/2}V_r^\top Q^{-1}A=Σr1/2​Vr⊤​Q−1. In practical numerical algebra one solves linear systems rather than forming explicit inverse matrices. The discarded whitened singular-value tail is an exact optimum for the regularized surrogate. It is not an exact optimum for full-network logit error or task loss.

    5. Regularization, factor orientation, and a usable algorithm

    The closed-form derivation assumed invertibility. Empirical moments can be singular, noisy, or numerically indefinite after accumulation. Appendix F applies shrinkage before Cholesky factorization:

    C‾=(1−α)C+αtr⁡(C)dId,αa=0.1,αg=0.3.(7)\overline C=(1-\alpha)C+\alpha\frac{\operatorname{tr}(C)}{d}I_d, \quad \alpha_a=0.1,\quad\alpha_g=0.3.\tag{7}C=(1−α)C+αdtr(C)​Id​,αa​=0.1,αg​=0.3.(7)

    If the Cholesky fails because of finite-precision negative eigenvalues, the paper shifts the diagonal by −λmin⁡+10−6-\lambda_{\min}+10^{-6}−λmin​+10−6 before retrying. Shrinkage trades directional specificity for conditioning. A small output eigenvalue can otherwise become a large unwhitening multiplier, amplifying both statistical error and floating-point error. The regularization is therefore part of the effective method, not an inconsequential numerical detail. The main comparisons hold it fixed across output-aware competitors.

    For Cholesky factors C‾a=LaLa⊤\overline C_a=L_aL_a^\topCa​=La​La⊤​ and C‾g=LgLg⊤\overline C_g=L_gL_g^\topCg​=Lg​Lg⊤​, the whitening expression is M=Lg⊤WLaM=L_g^\top W L_aM=Lg⊤​WLa​. Cholesky factors are triangular rather than symmetric; simply substituting LgL_gLg​ for a symmetric square root on the same side gives the wrong norm. The following pseudocode is my presentation of the paper’s mathematical procedure in the column convention, not a transcription of software.

    Algorithm 1. Compress one projection under fixed statistics

    1. Input W, input moment Ca, output Gram Cg, rank r.
2. Shrink Ca by alpha_a = 0.1 and Cg by alpha_g = 0.3.
3. Factor Ca = La La^T and Cg = Lg Lg^T;
   apply the stated diagonal repair if factorization fails.
4. Form M = Lg^T W La.
5. Compute the top r singular triplets Ur, Sr, Vr of M.
6. Solve Lg^T B = Ur sqrt(Sr).
7. Solve A La = sqrt(Sr) Vr^T.
8. Replace W a by B(A a); retain budget and evaluation records.

    Steps 6–7 give BA=Lg−⊤[Lg⊤WLa]rLa−1BA=L_g^{-\top}[L_g^\top W L_a]_rL_a^{-1}BA=Lg−⊤​[Lg⊤​WLa​]r​La−1​. The two factors can be computed for many ranks from a common spectrum after the statistics have been collected. This explains why the expensive calibration can be amortized across ratio sweeps. It does not imply that the best ranks are already known: deciding how much capacity to give each layer is a separate allocation problem.

    An alternative is to use pseudoinverses on supported subspaces. That can avoid imposing an isotropic floor but makes unobserved directions unconstrained and requires a policy for null-space components. Another alternative is diagonal weighting, which is cheaper but loses correlations between output channels. Complete-SVD chooses full factors because they retain those correlations while preserving an efficient closed-form rank constraint. The failure boundary is not only memory: poorly estimated small eigenvalues can make the theoretically sensible inverse transformation fragile.

    6. Why the output Gram appears intractable

    For causal sequences, a perturbation at input position ttt can affect logits at every t′≥tt'\ge tt′≥t. Let Hn,t,t′=∂hn,t′/∂sn,tH_{n,t,t'}=\partial h_{n,t'}/\partial s_{n,t}Hn,t,t′​=∂hn,t′​/∂sn,t​, where hhh is the last hidden state and WU∈RV×DW_U\in\mathbb R^{V\times D}WU​∈RV×D is the language-model head.     Using only the same-position Jacobian would be cheaper, but would omit the other causal descendants. Differentiating the sum of all output positions without any randomization is also different: it squares a sum rather than summing squares.     7. Random probes: an unbiased Gram with 64 backward passes

    Draw a Rademacher vector ψk∈{±1}V\psi_k\in\{\pm1\}^Vψk​∈{±1}V and project it through the head, zk=WU⊤ψkz_k=W_U^\top\psi_kzk​=WU⊤​ψk​. With K=64K=64K=64, the reverse-traversal count in the Llama 2 example is smaller than direct TVTVTV differentiation by exactly 1,024,000. This enormous ratio compares against an impractical exact construction. It should not be confused with a million-fold wall-clock improvement over a usable compression baseline.

    A scalar counterexample makes the sign requirement concrete. Suppose two future positions have projected derivatives g1=1g_1=1g1​=1 and g2=−0.8g_2=-0.8g2​=−0.8. The target sum of squares is 1+0.64=1.641+0.64=1.641+0.64=1.64. Uniform broadcast produces (1−0.8)2=0.04(1-0.8)^2=0.04(1−0.8)2=0.04, severely understating sensitivity. Independent signs give squared values 0.04, 3.24, 3.24 and 0.04 over four equally likely assignments, whose average is exactly 1.64. If both derivatives have the same sign, uniform broadcast can instead overstate sensitivity. There is no universal direction of bias.

    Appendix D.3 formalizes the omitted cross-position blocks. More sequences or more shared head probes do not automatically remove the deterministic cross-position contribution when all signs equal one. This is a different issue from Monte Carlo noise. Table 13 compares the exact Gram, uniform broadcast and signed broadcast on Pythia-410m. At retained ratios 0.90, 0.85 and 0.80, their WikiText-2 perplexities are respectively 23.4/23.8/23.6, 25.6/26.2/25.9, and 29.0/29.8/29.6. Signed broadcast closes part of the observed gap. That small-scale result supports the estimator design; it does not establish its variance or matrix accuracy at every large-model layer.

    Algorithm 2. Collect the Complete-SVD output statistic

    1. Fix calibration sequences X, the original model, and probe count K.
2. Draw K independent vocabulary Rademacher vectors psi_k.
3. Set z_k = WU^T psi_k; initialize each layer's Gram accumulator.
4. For each sequence n, run the original model and retain its graph.
5. For k = 1,...,K, draw independent signs at every output position.
6. Backpropagate sum_t' sign[n,t',k] * dot(z_k, h[n,t']).
7. At each target layer and input position t, accumulate g^T g.
8. Divide by total input tokens times K; shrink and factor the result.

    Statistics are collected at the original network. Outer products belong to each position’s layer-output gradient; forming an outer product only after summing gradients across input positions would introduce another unwanted set of cross terms. Reusing the same graph can reduce repeated forward work but requires memory management. The pseudocode describes the mathematics and leaves such engineering choices unspecified.

    8. KL-SVD: changing the probes changes the objective

    KL-SVD is a valuable control because it shares the factorization and estimator framework with Complete-SVD while changing the output kernel. At position t′t't′, draw Yn,t′(k)∼pn,t′Y_{n,t'}^{(k)}\sim p_{n,t'}Yn,t′(k)​∼pn,t′​ independently across positions and probes, then define

    zn,t′(k)=WU⊤(eYn,t′(k)−pn,t′).(12)z_{n,t'}^{(k)}=W_U^\top(e_{Y_{n,t'}^{(k)}}-p_{n,t'}). \tag{12}zn,t′(k)​=WU⊤​(eYn,t′(k)​​−pn,t′​).(12)

    The mean is zero. Its covariance is WU⊤[Diag⁡(p)−pp⊤]WUW_U^\top[\operatorname{Diag}(p)-pp^\top]W_UWU⊤​[Diag(p)−pp⊤]WU​. Different positions have independent centered draws, so cross-position terms already cancel without an auxiliary Rademacher sign. This differs from Complete-SVD: a shared centered head direction does not cancel cross-position products because the same random realization appears at both positions. Centering alone is insufficient; the relevant joint second moment determines the cancellation.

    The scalar differentiated in this construction is ∑t′(zn,t′(k))⊤hn,t′\sum_{t'}(z_{n,t'}^{(k)})^\top h_{n,t'}∑t′​(zn,t′(k)​)⊤hn,t′​, with each sampled zzz held fixed during differentiation. Although zzz was constructed from the original probabilities, differentiating through those probabilities would add terms that are not the intended cotangent-vector product. This stop-gradient requirement is mathematical, not a claim about any particular software.

    IO-SVD targets KL geometry but uses reductions that the paper distinguishes from random estimation [1, Appendix D.5.1]. First, a same-position approximation retains only t′=tt'=tt′=t and drops later causal outputs. Second, it keeps top-mmm probabilities and renormalizes. If their total mass is π\piπ and their original restricted vector is pSp_SpS​, the retained kernel is

    KIO=1πDiag⁡(pS)−1π2pSpS⊤=1π(KS−1−ππpSpS⊤).(13)K_{\mathrm{IO}}=\frac1\pi\operatorname{Diag}(p_S) -\frac1{\pi^2}p_Sp_S^\top =\frac1\pi\left(K_S-\frac{1-\pi}{\pi}p_Sp_S^\top\right). \tag{13}KIO​=π1​Diag(pS​)−π21​pS​pS⊤​=π1​(KS​−π1−π​pS​pS⊤​).(13)

    This is not simply the corresponding block of the full Fisher matrix. Since π\piπ changes with context, renormalization also changes relative context weights. Increasing the number of calibration samples does not fix those deterministic changes. Still, unbiasedness does not imply better task performance at every budget. On Mistral at CR 0.7, KL-SVD has higher perplexity than IO-SVD on all three corpora. On Llama 3 at the same ratio it improves C4 from 111.8 to 103.0 and PTB from 219.2 to 172.7. A reduction can have useful bias, and a correct estimator can have consequential variance.

    The clean experimental question is therefore twofold: which output geometry is useful, and how well can it be estimated under a fixed budget? Comparing Complete-SVD with KL-SVD largely isolates the first. Comparing exact, uniform and signed estimates at reduced scale addresses part of the second. Neither comparison completely separates nonlinear approximation error or the effect of covariance shrinkage.

    9. Main experiments: preserve the denominators

    The main setting uses 256 WikiText-2 sequences of length 2048. Tested text models include Mistral-7B, Llama 3-8B, Qwen3-8B and Llama 2-7B Base/Chat for additional experiments. The principal ratios 0.9 and 0.7 refer to the compressed attention and MLP projections; no such projection is left dense. Embeddings, the head and other storage mean these are not automatically whole-model storage ratios. The average accuracy in the main table aggregates six zero-shot datasets, while perplexity is reported on WikiText-2, C4 and PTB [1, Section 3].

    The paper reruns IO-SVD, while other baseline rows in Table 1 are reported from Thoma et al. The shared ranks and regularization improve comparability among output-aware methods, but the table is not an independent all-method rerun under identical executable conditions. I retain that provenance distinction when assessing small differences.

    Figure 5: Redrawn from paper Table 1 for Llama 3-8B at CR 0.7, WikiText-2 calibration and uniform ranks. Each panel has its own axis. Accuracy is the six-task zero-shot average; its higher-is-better direction differs from perplexity.

    For Llama 3-8B at CR 0.9, Complete-SVD reduces PTB perplexity from IO-SVD’s 30.9 to 23.0. My arithmetic is (30.9−23.0)/30.9=25.57%(30.9-23.0)/30.9=25.57\%(30.9−23.0)/30.9=25.57%. Against KL-SVD’s 27.1 it is a 15.13% reduction. At CR 0.7, the same model’s PTB values are 219.2, 172.7 and 123.7 for IO, KL and Complete. The reductions are 43.57% against IO and 28.37% against KL. Switching the comparator changes the headline substantially.

    Those gains do not make the heavily compressed model close to its original 9.9 PTB perplexity. Complete-SVD’s 123.7 remains about 12.49 times that baseline. On C4 at CR 0.7, 95.3 is better than 111.8 and 103.0, yet still about 10.03 times the original 9.5. Relative superiority under a severe compression setup and absolute deployment quality are separate questions.

    The tradeoff is visible in the same row: Complete-SVD has WikiText-2 PPL 16.8 versus KL-SVD’s 16.0, and accuracy 44.2% versus 45.6%. The latter is a 1.4 percentage-point gap, not a 1.4% relative gap. At CR 0.9, Complete’s 55.8% accuracy is also lower than KL’s 56.9%. The paper’s broad empirical claim is strongest for cross-corpus perplexity, not for a universal zero-shot improvement.

    With C4 calibration, Table 4 provides a useful distribution check on Llama 3-8B. At CR 0.9, Complete has C4 PPL 15.7 versus KL’s 15.5, but WikiText-2 12.8 versus 15.2 and PTB 17.3 versus 18.3. At CR 0.7, Complete is lower on all three perplexities: 51.2/36.2/89.9 versus 60.1/36.7/108.5. The result weakens a purely WikiText-specific explanation, while still using a very limited set of calibration and evaluation distributions.

    IFEval adds a different lens. Table 2 uses zero-shot prompt-level strict accuracy without a chat template; Llama 2 is the Chat variant. Complete exceeds KFAC-SVD and IO-SVD in all six settings. It exceeds KL-SVD in five, with the exception of Llama 2 Chat at CR 0.9: 26.6 versus 27.7. Mistral at CR 0.9 reaches 17.4 versus the original’s 16.8, but that single improvement cannot certify general instruction-following gains. Template choice, low absolute scores and the absence of uncertainty intervals limit that interpretation.

    10. Rank allocation can dominate the objective difference

    LEMS chooses how many directions to retain per layer; Complete-SVD chooses the directions under a specified layer budget. The paper follows the published LEMS procedure: one KL probe per layer compressed to 10% of its parameters, 32 calibration windows, a harmonic cross-layer term, ranks in multiples of eight, and an exact integer-program solve. Each method receives the allocation found under its own whitening. The depth prior is fixed at either γ=0\gamma=0γ=0 or γ=5\gamma=5γ=5 across methods. The LEMS authors had tuned per cell, which can produce stronger results than these fixed-prior comparisons.

    Figure 6: Redrawn from paper Tables 1 and 3. Left: PTB perplexity under LEMS at CR 0.7 and gamma 5. Right: Complete-SVD on Llama 3-8B, showing how allocation changes C4 and PTB perplexity relative to uniform ranks. These are paper observations, not new runs.

    At CR 0.9, LEMS with γ=0\gamma=0γ=0 brings Llama 3 Complete-SVD’s C4 PPL to 11.4, versus 22.4 with uniform ranks and 9.5 originally. At this budget, KL and Complete differ by very little, leaving little room for the objective to distinguish them. Under stronger compression, geometry matters again. At CR 0.7 and γ=5\gamma=5γ=5, Llama 3 PTB is 88.2 for KFAC, 73.1 for KL and 50.8 for Complete. Complete’s PTB reduction against KL is 30.51%, while its accuracy is 50.6% versus KL’s 51.5%.

    Within Complete-SVD, changing γ\gammaγ from zero to five changes Llama 3 PTB from 77.3 to 50.8 and C4 from 60.9 to 32.5. That hyperparameter effect is large enough to matter whenever comparing separate compression reports. A fair downstream comparison should freeze total parameter storage, alignment, calibration budget, rank-allocation policy and its tuning budget. “Same CR” alone does not establish equivalent rank patterns or equivalent search effort.

    The component ablation also supports the two-sided metric. Table 6 on Llama 2-7B at CR 0.5 gives WikiText-2 PPL 37.9 for input-only whitening, 2211.0 for output-only whitening and 22.4 for both; raw SVD is 53,670.8. This demonstrates complementarity in that setup. It does not imply that each covariance has the same marginal value at every ratio, or that the full metric would remain preferable if its calibration time were held against a cheaper method’s larger sample budget.

    11. Calibration cost, variance, storage, and throughput

    The reference budget is N=256N=256N=256, T=2048T=2048T=2048, K=64K=64K=64. Figure 2 varies sequence sample count and probe budget on Llama 2-7B at CR 0.8. The reference calibration-seed runs give PPL 7.53, 7.54 and 7.57; estimator-seed runs give 7.49, 7.53 and 7.55. Their standard deviations are about 0.02 and 0.03. The plotted uncertainty combines these as scal2+sest2≈0.04\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{cal}}^2+s_{\mathrm{est}}^2}\approx0.04scal2​+sest2​​≈0.04.

    Figure 7: From paper Fig. 2; Cropped from page 8 of v1. Left varies calibration samples with K = 64; right varies probes with N = 256. The error bars use the reference-budget seed variation described in the paper, not a separately estimated uncertainty at every plotted point.

    Reducing NNN from 256 to 32 raises PPL from 7.53 to 7.61. Varying KKK across 64, 128 and 256 changes it by at most 0.03. These observations make 64 a reasonable budget in this experiment. They do not show that eight probes would suffice, that long-context estimators behave similarly, or that the output covariance’s smallest eigenvalues are stable. A flat task score can coexist with substantial changes in matrix entries.

    Table 12 reports about 80 minutes on one H200 for Llama 3-8B and Mistral-7B. The listed stages are 12 minutes for the input moment, 64/61 minutes for the output Gram, and five minutes for SVD. The sums are 81 and 78 minutes, consistent with the rounded total. Statistics are reused across compression ratios. There is still a meaningful offline cost when the calibration distribution or model changes.

    The parameter-storage experiments must be read separately from rank and runtime. On Llama 2-7B, Table 8 reports CR 0.8 storage of 10.1 GB and PPL 7.5; adding GPTQ W3A16 gives 2.5 GB and PPL 9.2. At CR 0.6 the corresponding pairs are 7.7 GB/13.3 and 1.9 GB/16.7. Relative to the paper’s 13.5 GB FP16 model, 1.9 GB is 14.07% of the storage, approximately 7.11 times smaller. It is not the peak memory of a generation service: KV cache, activations, workspaces and runtime allocations are not included in that parameter-storage number.

    Appendix E’s remapping storage is another accounting regime. It combines overlapping factor submatrices at half precision to produce a ratio r/min⁡(da,ds)r/\min(d_a,d_s)r/min(da​,ds​). Results marked with an asterisk use that storage scheme. They should not be mixed with ordinary r(da+ds)/(dads)r(d_a+d_s)/(d_ad_s)r(da​+ds​)/(da​ds​) factor storage when translating ratio into rank. The paper reports the ordinary and remapped comparisons separately; a review should preserve the distinction rather than describe remapping as free rank capacity.

    Figure 8: From paper Fig. 3; Cropped from page 21 of v1. Panels show A100 batch scaling, A100 sequence-length scaling and Quadro P5000 batch scaling. OOM denotes a failed baseline configuration and does not define a finite speedup ratio.

    Appendix G reports larger relative gains at large batch and short sequence length. On the memory-limited P5000, the 40% model is about four times faster at batch 128. At batch 256 the original runs out of memory while the 40% and 60% models still run. These are useful feasibility and throughput observations. The paper does not provide enough serving detail to treat them as a TTFT/TPOT guarantee under an online workload. Two smaller matrix multiplications also introduce an intermediate activation and kernel overhead; parameter reduction alone cannot predict speedup for every shape.

    For an offline tradeoff, let compression cost CCC seconds and saved inference time per generated token be δ\deltaδ seconds under a specified workload. An illustrative amortization condition is

    Nbreak−even=C/δ.(14)N_{\mathrm{break-even}}=C/\delta.\tag{14}Nbreak−even​=C/δ.(14)

    Using the reported rounded C=4800C=4800C=4800 seconds and a hypothetical saving of 0.5 milliseconds per token gives 9.6 million tokens. This is my accounting example, not a measured break-even point. Parallelism, idle capacity, changes in batch size and monetary GPU rates would change the answer. The useful practice is to state those assumptions instead of declaring calibration negligible because it occurs offline.

    12. The multimodal appendix is a meaningful boundary

    Appendix H extends the study to LLaVA 1.5 7B/13B and SmolVLM 2B on ScienceQA-IMG and SEED-Bench-IMG. It also introduces Complete-SVDQ, which retains the factorization but uses QSVD’s sensitivity-aware allocation. Components are scored by singular value times the root-mean-square derivative of calibration loss with respect to that singular value. The retained set need not consist of the leading singular values. This is another reason to separate the geometry of each component from the allocation rule that decides which components survive.

    Table 15 does not support a blanket multimodal advantage. Its Avg. aggregates across the displayed tasks and compression ratios. On LLaVA 7B, IO-SVD has 63.85, Complete 62.06 and Complete-Q 63.03; on LLaVA 13B the values are 67.34, 67.12 and 67.06. On SmolVLM they are 75.58, 71.11 and 75.72. The QSVD allocator recovers much of the SmolVLM gap, but not a uniform win across all models. Different columns average different sets of tested ratios for SmolVLM versus LLaVA, so cross-model average differences are not a meaningful capability ranking.

    The main limitations section describes a 7–8B text-model scale boundary. The appendix includes a 13B VLM result, which should be acknowledged rather than silently omitted. It still does not establish the main text-model conclusions at large dense-model or mixture-of-experts scale, nor does it show free-form multimodal generation quality. Multiple-choice image benchmarks test a narrower behavior.

    13. Limitations of the current evidence

    The first limitation is the surrogate stack: local network linearization, a Kronecker curvature approximation, finite calibration, probe estimation, shrinkage and independent layer replacement. Each can move the selected subspace. Statistics from the original network do not capture the shifted activations or sensitivities caused by earlier compressed layers. A sequential or blockwise recalibration could address part of this mismatch, at extra cost. The paper itself identifies this issue and points to refinement-based alternatives.

    Second, most comparisons use a few text corpora and small sets of model sizes. Cross-corpus robustness is useful evidence, but C4 and PTB do not span code generation, multilingual rare tokens, long-context retrieval, conversational safety, or tool use. Even lower perplexity on both can coexist with regressions in specific decision margins. The zero-shot and IFEval results already show that metric choice changes which behaviors survive.

    Third, uncertainty is narrow. A handful of calibration/probe seed repeats on Llama 2 at one ratio does not establish the statistical significance of every difference in the main tables. Many small gaps would benefit from paired seeds and uncertainty intervals. The extreme uniform-rank configurations also suffer large absolute quality loss, so relative percentage improvements should not stand in for an application acceptance criterion.

    Fourth, inference evidence is not a complete service study. Reported tokens per second are not latency distributions, load-dependent goodput or end-to-end energy. Parameter storage is not runtime memory. The presence of some GPTQ combinations demonstrates compatibility in those settings; it does not establish an optimal joint rank/precision frontier or transfer to every low-bit kernel.

    Finally, the exact-reference test is at Pythia-410m and short sequence length 128, with 128 samples. It validates a tractable reference comparison in that regime. It cannot fully characterize the spectrum, estimator variance or structural curvature error of every 7–8B projection at 2048 tokens. Unbiasedness is a mathematically specific property with a specific conditioning set.

    14. Critical analysis: what I would change next

    My first priority would be an output-gauge ablation. The uniform-logit metric penalizes common shifts that leave probabilities unchanged. Let PV=I−11⊤/VP_V=I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/VPV​=I−11⊤/V. A centered-logit metric would replace the head Gram with

    Mcentered=WU⊤PVWU=WU⊤WU−(WU⊤1)(WU⊤1)⊤V.(15)M_{\mathrm{centered}}=W_U^\top P_VW_U =W_U^\top W_U-\frac{(W_U^\top\mathbf1)(W_U^\top\mathbf1)^\top}{V}. \tag{15}Mcentered​=WU⊤​PV​WU​=WU⊤​WU​−V(WU⊤​1)(WU⊤​1)⊤​.(15)

    This is my proposed experiment, not a method evaluated by the authors. It removes the softmax-invariant direction without probability weighting and can be formed without storing a V×VV\times VV×V matrix. Comparing Complete, centered Complete and KL under matched ranks, shrinkage and compute would test whether the empirical benefit comes from preserving rare relative logits or from a broader regularization effect. Failure of centering to help would also be informative: it could indicate that absolute-logit control indirectly stabilizes the multilayer approximation.

    Figure 9: Left is my analytical common-logit-shift example; no model was run. Right is redrawn from paper Table 15’s aggregate VLM accuracies. The two panels address different limits: an invariant output direction and a non-uniform empirical extension.

    My second priority is to measure the approximation stack separately. At reduced scale, compare the exact parameter-space Gram with its Kronecker approximation, then exact output factors with signed estimates, then the linearized output discrepancy with the actual compressed network. A directional error bound illustrates why this separation is useful:

    ∣v⊤(GW−G~W)v∣≤∥GW−G~W∥2∥v∥22.(16)|v^\top(G_W-\widetilde G_W)v| \le \|G_W-\widetilde G_W\|_2\|v\|_2^2. \tag{16}∣v⊤(GW​−GW​)v∣≤∥GW​−GW​∥2​∥v∥22​.(16)

    Probe count only addresses part of the difference in G~W\widetilde G_WGW​. Reporting matrix relative error, retained-subspace angles and actual output error alongside perplexity would reveal whether flat PPL-versus-KKK hides unstable whitening. The inverse transformation and truncated SVD are nonlinear, so an unbiased input Gram estimate does not produce an unbiased compressed weight.

    Third, I would allocate probe compute by sensitivity and conditioning rather than fixing KKK globally. A pilot budget could estimate uncertainty in the subspace near the rank cutoff; more probes would go to layers with small spectral gaps or unstable inverse directions. That proposal needs a fixed total wall-clock budget and a holdout calibration split. Without those controls, adaptive allocation can overfit the very proxy used to decide when estimation is sufficient.

    Fourth, I would make model selection explicitly multi-objective. Choose among Complete, KL or a trace-normalized mixture using held-out domain perplexity, task accuracy, generation checks and latency at the intended batch/length distribution. Trace normalization is necessary because IVI_VIV​ and the Fisher have very different total scales. The mixture is a suggested research direction, not an established improvement. A mixed-domain calibration set may be a simpler alternative; its effect should be measured before adding another tunable objective.

    Fifth, I would test inter-layer interaction with a modest blockwise recalibration experiment. Compress one block, recollect the next block’s statistics on the partially compressed model, and compare with original-network statistics under equal total cost. The hypothesis is that stronger compression makes stale statistics more damaging. The experiment should track cumulative error and task quality, because reducing a local mismatch does not guarantee a better global model.

    The operational implication is that deployment should start from an acceptance envelope. A model with a 28% relative PTB gain over another compressed model may still fail the minimum task score. Conversely, a small loss in an averaged zero-shot benchmark may be acceptable if the actual workload’s quality and memory constraints are met. The paper provides useful candidate objectives; the user’s workload still determines which tradeoff is worth buying.

    15. What a faithful follow-up study should record

    A useful reproduction would specify the exact paper/model versions, tokenization, calibration source and sampling seed, sequence length, head treatment, probe count and both sources of randomness. It should keep the input/output shrinkage strengths, diagonal repair rule and factor convention visible in the experiment protocol. A rank ratio needs a declared storage scheme, compressed projection set, alignment and allocator settings. Main-table provenance and each rerun should remain distinguishable.

    Evaluation should report the three perplexities separately, the six-task accuracy average with its constituent scores, IFEval’s template and strictness settings, and any application-specific generation acceptance tests. Runtime should include calibration time, parameter storage and peak memory separately; inference should declare hardware, batch, sequence length and the exact timing scope. An OOM observation is a capacity boundary, not a timing sample.

    For mathematical validation I independently checked a small weighted-SVD example, Cholesky orientation, the four-sign cancellation calculation, a categorical Fisher covariance identity and table arithmetic. These checks do not use or validate the authors’ implementation. No result in this review is represented as a newly trained model or a newly measured serving system. Official resources are useful entry points for a later study; I did not find a paper-specific released code link in the v1 paper or its arXiv record at reading time.

    16. Conclusion

    Complete-SVD makes a clear contribution: full-logit output geometry can be reduced to a two-sided weighted SVD, and its output factor can be estimated with a small, unbiased signed-probe construction. The head factorization and the cancellation of cross-position terms are the technical core. The strongest empirical case is improved cross-corpus perplexity under matched compression settings, supported by a KL counterpart that exposes a different quality preference.

    My takeaway is to keep three questions separate: what output behavior the objective values, what approximations make that objective tractable, and what quality/cost envelope the compressed model actually achieves. The paper answers the first two more completely than the third. Its results make uniform-logit matching a serious option to evaluate, while the accuracy tradeoff, gauge direction, stale statistics and multimodal appendix keep the conclusion appropriately bounded.
